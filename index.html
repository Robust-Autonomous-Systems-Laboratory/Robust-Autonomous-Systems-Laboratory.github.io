Current projects

Monitoring hibernating bats with autonomous-vehicle sensors

White-nose syndrome has killed millions of bats in North America. To help fight it, we are using sensors developed for autonomous vehicles (thermal, near-infrared, and color cameras) to count hibernating bats and map where they roost inside mines. One theory is that bats prefer colder parts of a mine, where white-nose syndrome is less likely to take hold. Knowing exactly where they roost lets us test that idea.

Lidar uncertainty modeling

How much can you trust a lidar range measurement, and how does that change in rain, snow, or fog? We are building tools for automotive lidar metrology: a Python application that extracts points on planar targets from recorded scans, trims noisy edge returns with iteratively reweighted least squares, and reports the repeatability (Type A uncertainty) of each sensor. Next, we will compare those baselines against data taken in adverse weather.
