Keweenaw Peninsula, Michigan · winters 2020–2025

WADS Winter Driving Data

The Winter Adverse Driving dataSet is a multi-sensor record of driving through lake-effect snow on the Keweenaw Peninsula: cameras, up to nine kinds of lidar, radar and GPS, recorded as ROS bags over four winters. This page summarizes what is in it so far, from automatic scene labels on the camera frames, the share of each lidar scan that is falling snow, and the weather at the nearest airport for every recording.

Camera labels

What the cameras saw

Every camera frame was sampled once per second and labelled with object boxes and scene tags by a vision-language model (Claude Haiku), and about 20,000 of the hardest frames got a second pass from a larger model (Claude Sonnet). The examples below show the second-pass labels. These are pre-labels for human review, not ground truth. People, faces and licence plates are blurred.

Lidar

Measuring snow in the point cloud

Falling snow shows up in lidar as isolated returns close to the sensor. A dynamic statistical outlier filter (DSOR: k = 5, std = 0.1, range multiplier = 0.1) flags them, and the share of returns it removes is a direct, per-second measure of how hard it is snowing at the car. Orange points below are the ones the filter removed from one 360° scan, 40 m around the vehicle.

Against public winter datasets

The same filter run on the full WADS collection and on two public datasets. Boreas (Toronto) records mostly clear or lightly snowing air; its one snowstorm looks like a typical WADS scan.

Weather · KCMX

Conditions on each recording day

Every bag carries the NWS surface observations from Houghton County Memorial Airport (KCMX) during the recording, plus the National Blend of Models snowfall forecast issued at least an hour before it. Temperature, humidity and pressure here are from the report nearest each bag; forecast snow is the day's total from the last forecast run before the drive.

The airport is one point. Lake-effect snow along a drive can be much heavier or lighter than at KCMX, which is why the lidar measure above matters. Source: Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS and NBM archives.

Pipeline

How the data is processed

Index

Bags are read through their trailing index only, so a 100 GB bag can be cataloged in seconds. Drives are grouped by time, and duplicate copies are detected.

Label

One frame per second per camera, labelled with boxes and scene tags; hard frames get a second opinion. Results are cached and reproducible.

Lidar snow

DSOR runs on one scan per second for every lidar, and filtered copies of every bag are written alongside the originals.

Weather

Airport observations and archived forecasts are attached to each bag as metadata.

Georeference

In progress: placing the 2021 drives, which have no GPS, from lidar odometry, image matches to other years and the road network. Validation on a 2022 drive with its GPS hidden: 3.7 m median error.

Numbers on this page are computed from the full collection on the lab's bag server (237 unique bags). Model labels are automatic and will contain errors.
